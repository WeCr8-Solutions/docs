A blocked job that nobody knows about is the most expensive kind. Jobline.ai gives you structured ways to flag blockers, assign owners, and get notified before a delay turns into a missed delivery.

## What is a blocker?

A blocker is any condition that prevents a job from moving forward. Common examples:

- Waiting on raw material that has not arrived
- Missing or broken tooling
- Parts on hold pending QA inspection
- Machine is down waiting on maintenance
- Program has not been written or proven out yet
- Operator is not available

Blockers are recorded by setting the job state to the appropriate waiting or down state, and selecting a delay code that describes the cause.

## Delay codes

When you put a job on hold or into a down state, select the delay code that best matches the root cause:

| Code | Use when |
|---|---|
| **None** | No delay — job is running normally |
| **Material** | Waiting on raw material or stock |
| **Tooling** | Missing, broken, or unordered tooling |
| **QA** | Waiting on inspection or NCR disposition |
| **Programming** | CNC program not ready or needs revision |
| **Maintenance** | Machine down waiting on maintenance |
| **Setup** | Setup is taking longer than expected |
| **Operator** | No qualified operator available |
| **Inspection** | Waiting on inspection (first article or in-process) |
| **Shipping** | Waiting on inbound or outbound shipping |
| **Unknown** | Cause has not been identified yet |

## How to report a blocker

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set the job state">
    From the work order, station card, or handoff form, change the job state to the appropriate status:

    - **Waiting on Material**
    - **Waiting on Tooling**
    - **Waiting on QA**
    - **Machine Down / Issue**
    - **On Hold**
  </Step>

  <Step title="Select the delay code">
    Choose the delay code that describes why the job is blocked. This is required — it is how downtime and blocker trends are tracked.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add notes">
    Add a description of the issue in the handoff notes or the work order notes field. Be specific: what is missing, who has been contacted, and what the expected resolution is.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Issue and follow-up items

Beyond blocking a job, you can create individual issue items — either from a handoff form or directly on a work order — to track specific action items that need to be resolved.

Each issue item includes:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Issue description | What the problem or task is |
| Action required | What needs to happen to resolve it |
| Owner | Who is responsible for taking action |
| Priority | Low, Medium, High, or Critical |
| Due by | When the item must be resolved |

<Tip>
  Always assign an owner and due date to every issue or follow-up item. Unowned action items get lost.
</Tip>

## Priority levels

Priority affects how jobs are sorted in the queue and how they appear on dashboards:

| Priority | When to use |
|---|---|
| **Low** | Nice to address, but not time-sensitive |
| **Medium** | Should be resolved soon, won't cause an immediate problem |
| **High** | Needs attention today — risk of missing schedule |
| **Critical** | Needs attention right now — actively blocking delivery or safety |

High and Critical jobs float to the top of the queue and are highlighted on the supervisor dashboard.

## Smart alerts

Smart alerts notify you automatically when conditions exceed configured thresholds:

- A job is past its due date
- A machine enters alarm state
- Scrap rate exceeds a configured threshold for a station or part

Thresholds are configured by your supervisor or administrator in settings.

## Notification channels

When an alert fires, you receive a notification through:

- **In-app** — a notification badge and alert panel within Jobline.ai
- **Email** — an email to your registered address with the alert details

Notification preferences can be adjusted in your account settings.
