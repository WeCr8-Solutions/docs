title: "Downtime Tracking"
description: "Record machine downtime events with reason codes to identify recurring problems and measure their impact."
---

Every minute a machine is down costs money. Downtime tracking captures machine-down events with reason codes and links them to specific work orders so you can see exactly how much time is being lost, to what, and on which machines.

<Note>
  Downtime data is aggregated per station. Review it weekly to find your biggest recurring problems.
</Note>

## What downtime tracking captures

Each downtime event records:

- **Machine / station** — which machine was down
- **Reason code (delay code)** — the cause of the downtime
- **Duration** — how long the machine was in the down state
- **Associated work order** — which job was affected
- **Notes** — any additional context from the operator or supervisor

## How to report a machine-down event

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set the job state to 'Machine Down / Issue'">
    When your machine goes down, update the job state on the work order or station card to **Machine Down / Issue**. This starts the downtime clock.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Select the delay code">
    Choose the delay code that best describes why the machine is down:

    - **Maintenance** — unplanned breakdown or waiting on maintenance technician
    - **Tooling** — broken tool, missing tooling, or tooling needs replacement
    - **Operator** — no qualified operator available to run the machine

    Other delay codes (Material, QA, Programming, etc.) are also available if the stoppage is not a true machine-down event but the job still cannot proceed.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add notes in the handoff">
    When you fill out your shift handoff, include details about the machine-down event in the notes field: what happened, who was contacted, what was done, and whether it is resolved or still open.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Clear the state when resolved">
    When the machine is back up and the job can continue, update the job state back to **Part Running**, **Setup in Progress**, or whichever state is appropriate. This closes the downtime event.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Trend analysis

The downtime dashboard lets you review machine-down history across your shop:

- **By machine** — see which stations have the most downtime events or the longest total downtime
- **By reason code** — see whether your biggest problem is maintenance, tooling, or something else
- **By time period** — compare this week to last week, or this month to last month

Filter combinations let you ask specific questions — for example: "How much time has CNC Lathe #2 lost to maintenance issues in the last 30 days?"

## How downtime data helps your shop

Reviewing downtime trends regularly lets you:

- **Identify machines with recurring problems** — if the same machine goes down for maintenance every two weeks, it is telling you something
- **Justify maintenance investments** — data showing 40 hours of lost production per quarter makes the case for a scheduled rebuild or replacement
- **Quantify your biggest time-wasters** — supervisors can use this data in team meetings to focus improvement efforts where they will have the most impact
- **Build a case for tooling upgrades** — if tool breakage is causing repeated downtime on a specific part or operation, the data shows it clearly
